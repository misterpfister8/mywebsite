Wisperpfister · iPhone, iPad and Mac

Privacy.

Last updated: 14 October 2026. Applies to the Wisperpfister apps for iPhone and iPad and for Mac. This is a translation; the German version is binding.

Controller: Kilian Pfister, Switzerland. Contact: wisperpfister@misterpfister.net

In short

Wisperpfister turns speech into text on your device. The apps have no account, no analytics and no ads. Your dictations, your dictionary and your history stay on your device and, where stated below, in your own device backup. The audio and text of your dictations are not sent to me or to anyone else. I only receive what you send me yourself: feedback via TestFlight, the report from “Diagnose kopieren…” (Copy diagnostics) or an email.

What the apps do

iPhone and iPad. Everything stays on your iPhone or iPad. Recognition and clean-up run on the device. The only thing that goes online is downloading speech models: iOS downloads Apple’s speech model from Apple the first time, and the app fetches Parakeet from Hugging Face when you start that download.

Mac. Everything stays on this Mac. Recognition and AI clean-up run on the device; the app talks to Ollama only via the local address 127.0.0.1. The only thing that goes online is downloading speech models: macOS downloads Apple’s speech model from Apple the first time, and the app fetches Parakeet from Hugging Face when you start that download. The microphone is only open during a recording. To check that the text has arrived, the app briefly reads the field, in memory only.

Microphone

On iPhone and iPad the microphone is open during a session so that the keyboard can start a dictation immediately; iOS shows this with the orange dot. Only what is spoken between start and stop is kept, plus 0.3 seconds before it. The session ends after the time you set (at most 8 hours), in the app, in the Live Activity or when you answer a call. On the Mac the microphone is only open while you are recording.

Downloading speech models

If Apple’s speech model for your language is missing, the operating system downloads it from Apple; Apple’s privacy policy applies to that. The app downloads Parakeet only when you start it in the settings, from Hugging Face (Hugging Face, Inc., USA). In doing so, Hugging Face learns your IP address and which files are requested, as with any download. Your device connects directly; I receive nothing from it. Hugging Face is based in the USA, and Hugging Face’s privacy policy (huggingface.co/privacy) applies to this download. Switzerland and the EU recognise data protection in the USA only for companies that take part in the Data Privacy Framework; I have not established whether Hugging Face is one of them. If you do not want a connection to Hugging Face, do not download Parakeet: the apps then use Apple’s speech recognition.

What is stored

Your settings and your dictionary stay in the app and are part of your device backups (iCloud or computer backup on iPhone or iPad, Time Machine on the Mac). History is off until you turn it on; the app then stores the raw text, cleaned-up text, language, duration and time on this device only, not in the backup, and deletes entries after the period you set (7 days by default), at the latest the next time the app is opened. On iPhone and iPad the last dictation is kept for at most 30 minutes (not in the backup; if the app is not running then, until it is next opened) so that it is not lost if the app quits. On Mac, iPhone and iPad the daily statistics count only dictations, words and recording seconds, never the text, and stay on the device, not in the backup; you can turn them off or reset them, and both delete them. On the Mac the data is stored in ~/Library/Application Support/Wisperpfister.

Keyboard (iPhone and iPad)

The keyboard receives dictations, dictionary terms and settings through a keychain item that only the app and its keyboard can read. It does not leave the device and is not synced with iCloud; a dictation is available there for at most two minutes. For this the keyboard needs “Full Access”. It does not store or transmit what you type; it keeps a copy of your dictionary terms and its settings, on this device. For suggestions, iOS gives the keyboard the names of your contacts and your text replacements; they stay on the device, and the keyboard does not store them. If you delete the app, the dictionary terms and settings remain in the device’s keychain until iOS resets it; dictations are gone from there after two minutes anyway.

Clipboard

On iPhone and iPad, a dictation that you start in the app and that finishes while the app is open is copied to this device’s clipboard, not to your other devices, and iOS deletes it there after 5 minutes. Anything you copy yourself with “Kopieren” (Copy) is treated by iOS like any other copy and may be shared with your other devices. On the Mac the app briefly puts the text on the clipboard to paste it and then restores the previous contents.

Learning (Mac)

If “Korrekturen vorschlagen” (Suggest corrections) is on, the app reads the field for at most 8 seconds after pasting to detect a changed word, and asks whether it should remember it. Only what you confirm is stored.

Command mode (Mac)

If you hold ⇧ while pressing the key, the app reads the selected text in the field, passes it with your instruction to the AI on this Mac (Apple Intelligence or your Ollama via the local address) and replaces the selection with the answer. The selected text goes into neither the history nor the statistics.

App Store

Downloading from the App Store means: Apple processes your purchase (the app is free), ratings and, if you allow it in your device settings, anonymous usage and crash reports, which I see in App Store Connect. I receive neither your name nor your email address. Apple’s privacy policy applies to that. If you email me, I use the email only to reply to you and delete it once the matter is settled.

Beta via TestFlight

During the beta I distribute the iPhone and iPad app through Apple’s TestFlight. Apple gives me the name and email address of the testers I invite (for those who join via a public link, only what they share themselves), device type and system version, and crash reports and feedback with screenshots if you send them. I use this only to find bugs and delete it when the beta ends, or earlier if you ask. Apple’s privacy policy applies to TestFlight itself. From Mac testers I only receive what they send me themselves, such as the report from “Diagnose kopieren…” (Copy diagnostics).

Your rights

You can request information about the data I hold about you (at most the TestFlight details mentioned above and what you send me), and its correction or deletion. Write to wisperpfister@misterpfister.net. I process this data only to reply to you, run the beta and fix bugs; for people in the EU this is based on Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR. Where the law provides for it, you can also object to the processing and request your data in a common format. You can lodge a complaint with the Swiss Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC, edoeb.admin.ch), and in the EU also with the data protection authority of your country.

Changes

If what the apps do with data changes, I will update this policy and change the date at the top.
